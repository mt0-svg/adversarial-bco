One program of a random access machine with logarithmic cost and a weak membership oracle for adversarial bandit convex optimisation: on every convex body given by the oracle and two rational balls, against an adversary that chooses each loss from the whole past and with a subgaussian noise, it plays each round in a number of bit operations polynomial in d, ln n and the bit lengths of δ and of the balls, and its regret is at most a constant times d^{3/2} sqrt(n) ln^5(2dn) + d sqrt(n ln(2/δ)) ln^{9/2}(2dn) with probability at least 1 − δ (Theorem 1.5); for d ≥ 4·10^6, n ≥ d^{10}, δ < 1/2 and ln(1/δ) ≤ n/d^6, the minimax (1 − δ)-quantile of the regret is at least a constant times d^{3/2} sqrt(n)/ln d + d sqrt(n ln(1/δ))/(ln d sqrt(ln n)) on every such body, and the program attains it up to a factor polylogarithmic in d and n (Theorems 1.6 and 1.7). On the rational polytopes of Bubeck, Eldan and Lee a program of the same machine without an oracle has expected regret at most a constant times d^{3/2} sqrt(n) ln^5(2dn), which with the lower bound on the cube proves their Conjecture 1, with time counted in bit operations (Corollary 1.8). The rate d^{3/2} sqrt(n) up to logarithms against an adaptive adversary was obtained first by Bakhtiari, Jamieson, Lattimore and Ratliff (arXiv:2611.33531).
